An interactive study of predictive maintenance on a simulated distribution network

When does a fault prediction deserve to be believed?

An interactive study of predictive-maintenance detector architectures, run on a simulated 10 kV distribution network: twenty-four transformers, four feeders, three seeded faults, and five architectures assembled from the centre's own publications. Every mechanism is mapped to its source in the provenance section.

The work this page builds on

Section 2 · Interactive simulation

The network, observed for 300 days

A simulated 10 kV distribution network runs through 300 days while detectors watch every transformer. Three things to know before pressing play:

  1. The diagram is a small electricity grid. Every twin-circle symbol is a transformer, coloured by its true condition: green is healthy, amber is a developing fault, red is damage. Three of the twenty-four develop real faults during the run.
  2. The selected detector files maintenance tickets. The small triangles on the diagram are its tickets. The chips below switch between detector designs, from a bare temperature limit to the centre's full gated architecture.
  3. The three tiles score the run. How many of the three real faults were caught, how many days before damage, and how many tickets were false alarms. The right panel tells the story of whichever transformer you select.

Terms used on this page
expected temperature · what a model trained on the first 60 healthy days predicts for today's load and weather
residual · the measured temperature minus the expected one
z · the residual divided by its normal day-to-day variation; values above about 3 are unusual
accumulated evidence (CUSUM) · a running total of suspicion: it grows while readings stay unusual and drains when they return to normal
ensemble disagreement · fifteen models trained on resamplings of the same data; when they stop agreeing, today's conditions are unlike the training data
lead time · days between a detector's first ticket on an asset and the day its fault becomes damage
false alarm · a ticket on a transformer that was in fact healthy
regime change · the world stops resembling the training data; here, a heat wave followed by growing electric-vehicle charging
censoring · a cable that was replaced early or still alive when observation ended; its full lifetime was never seen

Limitations

The network, loads and faults are simulated, and the page says so rather than pretending to utility data. The thermal model is a simplified IEC 60076-7 shape. Bootstrap ridge ensembles stand in for the deep models in the cited papers, and ensemble disagreement stands in for MC-dropout: the claim being demonstrated is the uncertainty logic, not the architecture. District heating is deliberately left out to keep one readable grid; the same voting, survival and explainability machinery is the centre's district heating agenda, and the demo would carry over.

A caveat worth stating plainly: five seeds, one simulator, written by the applicant. The page demonstrates reasoning and engineering practice, not utility-grade measurement.
